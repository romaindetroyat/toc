# Phase 1 Plan 49: Le son de l'iPhone en mode silencieux Summary

**The first gesture now asks for the « playback » audio session before it creates the AudioContext, and on an iPhone or iPad it also starts a hidden, looping, silent <audio> element whose WAV is generated by the code (a data URI: no file, no network). That element puts the page in the playback category, which the ringer switch does not mute. It is paused when the page is hidden and while « Son » is off. The help texts no longer tell Romain to leave silent mode. Whether the iPhone really sounds with its switch on silent can only be checked on his phone (01-54).**

## What was built

### Task 1 (tracer), 9bb133a: keepAlive.ts, the unlock order, the wiring, audio.spec

- `keepAlive.ts`: `silentWavDataUri()` builds a 44-byte RIFF/WAVE header (PCM, mono, 16-bit, 8 000 Hz) and 4 000 zero samples (0.5 s), in base64. `isAppleTouch()` matches iPhone, iPad and iPod user agents, and an iPad on iPadOS (`MacIntel` with `maxTouchPoints > 1`). `createKeepAlive()` creates the element once (or reuses the one in the page): `loop`, `preload="auto"`, `playsinline`, `x-webkit-airplay="deny"`, `disableRemotePlayback`, `aria-hidden`, `hidden`, `data-audio-keepalive`, no controls. `start()` swallows a refused `play()`. The header comment gives the reason (the ringer switch, the playback category) and Romain's words, including « Oui, ça me va ».
- `engine.ts` `unlock()`: the audio session is set first. Order check (awk over `unlock`): `session.type = "playback"` at line 453, `new WebAudioBackend()` at line 456 (before 01-49 it came after the engine was created). Then `armKeepAlive()` runs once the engine exists: on Apple touch WebKit it creates the element, listens to `visibilitychange` (hidden → pause), and starts it when the page is visible and « Son » is on. The retry listeners (pointerup, touchend, click, keydown) restart it under the same conditions. `setMix` pauses it when « Son » goes off and starts it when « Son » comes back.
- `debug.ts`: the types of `__tocDebug.audio.keepAlive` and `audioSessionType`.
- `keepAlive.test.ts` (8 cases): the WAV header fields, its length and silence, the custom length and rate, the three detections, and the element's attributes, play and pause, a refused play, and reuse (on a fake document; vitest runs in node).
- `audio.spec.ts`, 4 new tests. An init script installs a fake `navigator.audioSession { type: "auto" }` when the browser has none, and wraps `AudioContext` to record the session type at each construction.
  - After « Jouer la partie », the type recorded is `["playback"]` on both projects. On the phone project the element exists, loops, has a `data:audio/wav;base64,UklGR…` source, is not visible, has no controls, denies AirPlay and plays. On the desktop project it does not exist.
  - A game opened directly on `#/partie`: the first tap gives the same result.
  - Phone only: the hidden page pauses it, coming back leaves it paused, and the next tap plays it again. « Son » off in Réglages pauses it, a tap elsewhere does not restart it, and « Son » back on plays it again.

### Task 2, 51d2733: the help texts and the audio contract

- `reglages.spec.ts` and `sons.spec.ts` asserted the old start « Pas de son sur iPhone »; they now assert the whole new sentence (only that string changed).

**3. [Design choice within the plan] « Son » back on restarts the element at once**
- The plan says the next gesture restarts it after « Son » off. The tap on the switch is itself that gesture, but Svelte applies `setMix` in an effect after the click listeners have run. So `setMix` itself restarts the element when « Son » comes back on and the page is visible. The return from the background still waits for the next tap, as the plan says. Tested in audio.spec.
